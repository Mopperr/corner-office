# Why they are moving

Twenty households on the job board, each with a reason, told by the people
themselves while you work (`data/stories.json`, said by `scripts/client_party.gd`).
The reason is on the clipboard; three parts of the story come out as the room
fills; the last line closes the results card.

## Ilse Brandt — Ilse's bungalow

*widow, 81, warm, moving into the bungalow a (elder).*

Walter died in the spring. The big house had too many stairs and too many of his coats.

- "Forty-six years in the old place. I kept walking into his study to tell him things."
- "The clock was his father's. It loses four minutes a day and I set it back every morning. I'll keep doing that."
- "My daughter wanted me nearer. This is nearer. It has one floor and a garden I can manage, and that's enough."

*Ilse sits in Walter's chair, looks at the window for a while, and says that will do nicely.*

## The Okonkwos — The Okonkwos' move

*family of four, soon five, warm, moving into the semi a (adult, adult, kid, kid).*

A third child is due in March and the flat had two bedrooms and one argument a night about them.

- "We found out in August. Grace said 'we need a house' before she said 'congratulations'. She was right."
- "The boys have shared a room since Tobi was born. Tobi says he is never sharing again. He will share with the baby."
- "The piano was Grace's mother's. Nobody plays it. It is coming anyway, because it is Grace's mother's."

*Grace puts a hand on the piano, the boys fight over the back bedroom, and the Okonkwos are home.*

## Maeve and Tom — Maeve and Tom's first house

*first-time buyers, fussy, moving into the terrace a (adult, adult).*

Six years of renting, three landlords, one deposit never returned. The terrace is theirs, every creaking inch.

- "We viewed it in the rain. Tom said the damp patch was 'character'. It was damp. It's fixed now. Mostly."
- "The last landlord kept our deposit for a mark on a wall that was there when we moved in. I have photos. I have so many photos."
- "Tom labelled the box with the kettle in it 'KETTLE, URGENT'. That one we're opening first."

*Tom finds the box marked KETTLE, URGENT. Maeve finds the mugs. Nobody can take the house off them.*

## The Reyes family — The Reyes family's big move

*family of three, from the coast, warm, moving into the detached a (adult, adult, kid).*

Marco's job moved inland and the whole family came with it, one sulking eleven-year-old included.

- "Lucia hasn't spoken since the motorway. She left her best friend by the sea. We've promised a trip back every month."
- "The company paid for the move. We didn't pay for a crew this good, so thank you. I mean it."
- "There's a bedroom with a window seat. We haven't told Lucia it's hers yet. We're saving it."

*Lucia finds the window seat on her own. She doesn't say anything, but she stays there, and that's a yes.*

## Priya — Priya's flat

*junior doctor, fussy, moving into the flat b (adult).*

Night shifts at the hospital and a forty-minute bus. The flat is four minutes on foot and that is the entire reason.

- "I finished a thirteen-hour shift at seven this morning. Please don't tell me the box numbers twice."
- "My mother wanted me to buy a house with a garden. I want to sleep. Sleep won."
- "The bed goes against the wall with no window. I measured the light at six a.m. I'm not joking."

*Priya closes the curtains, tests the bed, and is asleep before you've shut the van.*

## The Lindgrens — The Lindgrens' townhouse

*family of three, fussy, moving into the townhouse b (adult, adult, kid).*

Aunt Britt left them the townhouse in her will, with a note that said 'fix the roof and don't sell it'.

- "Britt lived here fifty years. She left the house to us and her cat to the neighbour. The cat got the better deal, she said."
- "The roof is fixed. Cost more than the move. Britt would have said we were robbed and she'd have been right."
- "Elsa's grandad built the train set. It has never been fully set up. The loft is the right size. That's not a coincidence."

*Elsa's grandad's train runs a full loop in the loft for the first time in thirty years. Britt would have approved of the roof.*

## Mr Bello — Mr Bello's cottage

*retired gardener, 77, rude, moving into the cottage a (elder).*

He fell off a ladder in his own orchard and his daughter said that was that. The cottage is two streets from her.

- "Forty years I had that orchard. One fall and suddenly I'm 'a worry'. I'm not a worry, I'm a man with a bruise."
- "She picked this place. Nice shed, I'll give her that. Garden's a handkerchief. I'll make it work."
- "Don't put the mower near the paint. The mower has never been near the paint. I have standards, even now."

*Mr Bello inspects the shed, moves the mower two inches, and says 'fine' in a way that means thank you.*

## Jun and Sol — Jun and Sol's loft

*designers, fussy, moving into the loftflat a (adult, adult).*

The studio got three big clients in a year and the kitchen table stopped being an office.

- "The brick wall is why we took it. Everything else is why we nearly didn't."
- "The telescope was Jun's dad's. It's not for stars. It's for pretending we can see the river from here."

*Sol moves one chair a quarter-turn, Jun says nothing, and the loft is officially a studio.*

## The Varga family — The Vargas move in with Grandad

*family of four plus Grandad, rude, moving into the villa a (adult, adult, kid, kid, elder).*

Grandad can't run the villa on his own any more and won't leave it, so the whole family is moving in with him.

- "Dad built this house. Dad's not leaving this house. So here we all are, in Dad's house. Mind the paintwork, he'll notice."

- "The shed's his. The loft's ours. The kitchen is a negotiation we haven't had yet."

*Grandad stands at the top of his stairs, counts everyone in, and says the house finally sounds right.*

## Mrs Castellano — Mrs Castellano's new start

*widow, 68, warm, moving into the semi b (elder).*

Forty years in the family home. After Enzo, the rooms were too quiet and the garden too big.

- "The children were raised in the old house. I kept it two years after Enzo. I was keeping it for him, not for me."
- "I gave the dining table to my son. Twelve chairs. I need two."
- "The photographs go in the lounge, all of them, even the ugly one from 1979. Especially that one."

*Mrs Castellano puts the photograph from 1979 where she can see it from the kitchen, and the house stops being empty.*

## Dev and the boys — Dev and the boys

*single dad, two boys, rude, moving into the terrace b (adult, kid, kid).*

The divorce went through in June. The terrace is small, near the school, and his.

## The Haddads — The Haddads come home

Ten years overseas for Samir's work. Teta is eighty-four and wanted to come home, so they all came home.

## Hal Okafor — Hal comes back to town

- "Left this town at eighteen. Said I'd never come back. The shop's sold, the knees are gone, here I am."
