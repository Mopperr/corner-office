## (a) What format finished models must go in

**There is currently no way to give a catalogue piece a .glb.**
- `tools/check_items.py` line 57: `model` **must** start with `proc:`, and the props entry must have at least 2 parts. The smoke test (`_catalog_checks`) also reads every piece's `catalog_props` and fails if there are fewer than 2 parts.
- `Props.make(spec)`: `proc:<id>` goes to `_proc` → `_proc_from_data`, which reads `data/props.json`, then `ItemDB.catalog_props`. Any other spec loads `res://assets/models/<spec>.glb`. GLBs whose name starts with `hh_` keep up to **7** materials and get `dress_household` textures by mesh name (cushion/seat/towel→linen, rug/mat→kilim, top→oak, tray→steel, box→cardboard, soil→concrete). Everything else, **including every `proc:` catalogue piece**, goes through `cap_prop_materials(node, 2)`.
- Nothing scales a GLB to the item's w/d/h. It must be built at real size, in metres.
- **Recommended route (needs a small code hook from Claude; we can't add it without editing existing files):** Art delivers one GLB per family, `assets/models/hh_cat_<family_id>.glb`, built at the plain variant's w/d/h (see csv). Origin at the bottom centre, front toward −Y in Blender (+Z in Godot), flat Principled materials (roughness about 0.82), and meshes named body/door/seat/cushion/top/etc. so the textures apply. The recolourable colour-way surface uses a material named `way`, and wood or metal stays in fixed palette materials. Claude adds about 10 lines in `Props.make`: for `proc:<id>`, if `hh_cat_<family>.glb` exists, instance it, scale it by the item's w/d/h over the family's base size, and tint the `way` material to the item's colour-way hex. Otherwise fall back to the primitives. The `proc:` model ids and props stay in place, so check_items and smoke keep passing, and it's add-only for us.
- Alternatives: a GLB for every piece (1,339 files, too many), or changing `model` to a GLB name (breaks check_items and smoke, and edits Claude's files).

## (b) Current art style and palette

- Chunky, low-poly, toy-like dioramas. Furniture GLBs are made in Blender from bevelled boxes, cylinders and cones (`tools/build_household.py`: bevel 4 to 15 mm, 18-segment cylinders, 16x10 spheres), with flat matte colour (no texture maps in the GLB). Godot then adds a light specular polish and the team's triplanar textures on named parts.
- Catalogue palette (gen_items.py): oak C68B59, walnut 6B4A3A, pine D9B98A, dark 2B2622, cream F4F1EA, white EAE6DC, card E7DCC6, terracotta D9765F, sage 8FA882, mustard E6B655, blue 4A6FA5, rose E8B4A8, night 3E3A4F, red B6453F, green 5E7B52, steel C9CED3, grey 8E8276, teal 4F8A8B, plum 6E4B6E, brass C9A24E, kraft C9A070, glass BFD6DA, black 1E1B19. Off palette: copper B87333 (46 parts) and A67B4C (3).
- UI palette (`scripts/ui_kit.gd`): cream F4E9D8, wood C68B59, dark 6B4A3A, card D9A86C, sage 8FAF7E, terra D9765F, mustard E6B655, blue 7FA3B8, night 3E3A4F, pink E8B4A8. build_household.py uses slightly different woods (pine C9A27E, oak 8E6E5A, walnut 5A3B2A).

## (c) Visibility ranking (method)

This is an estimate of expected placements when every house (20 in `data/homes.json`) and every non-garage venue (8) is visited once. It is computed from the code paths:
1. **House furniture** (`Homes.owner_layout`): each room draws up to 4 floor pieces from `room_pool([tag],[floor])` with w≤1.3 and d≤0.9, uniformly by piece.
2. **House-move cargo** (`JobGen._recipe_home`): about 10 picks, uniform over every content item for the house's rooms (old + catalogue).
3. **Shop/office clutter** (`Diorama._dress_clutter`): 3 to 7 floor and 2 to 4 hang pieces from `CLUTTER_TAGS` rooms (default office+living), w≤1.4, h≤1.9.
Families with more variants win more picks. Floor pieces dominate because they show in both furniture and clutter. Small pieces appear only as move cargo, and office families only in the 8 venues, so they rank lower even though every shop shows them every visit.
