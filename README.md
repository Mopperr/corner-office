# Corner Office

A cozy PC game about packing an old office, trading odd goods on a beige computer, and moving other people's things. The first playable slice is Dana's Garage Startup, the Whisker & Bean cafe job, a rainy drive, Grandpa's Train Shop, a duck-pond loop, and the Bakery Loft unlock.

## Run

Install [Godot 4.7](https://godotengine.org/) or newer (the project was made with 4.7.2). Open this folder in the Godot editor, or from a terminal:

```bash
godot --path . 
```

The project uses the Forward+ renderer and Jolt Physics. The UI base is 1920×1080 with stretch aspect `expand`, and body text stays at least 18px so it remains readable on a 1280×800 Deck. The main menu looks into the garage through an orthographic camera (yaw 45°, pitch -30°, size 7.2). If `game/menu_room_loft.json` is present, that shot is the Bakery Loft instead. It drifts once every 24 seconds, and that drift stays off when the system asks for reduced motion. The main scene is `res://scenes/main.tscn`. Headless logic check (import the class cache first, or a fresh run sits there until Godot finishes that scan):

```bash
godot --headless --path . --import --quit
godot --headless --path . --quit-after 240 res://tests/smoke.tscn
```

`tests/run_venues.sh` builds every job venue and every rung of the shop ladder headless and checks each comes up dressed (`VENUES_OK`); with `CO_SHOTS` set and a renderer it takes a picture of each. `tests/run_smoke.sh` runs those two steps and checks for `SMOKE_OK` (it also checks that the job board is deterministic, playable, and saved, and that goals pay out). `tests/run_playthrough.sh` then plays the whole slice the way a player would (menu, garage, computer, map, the three jobs through pack, truck, drive, unpack and results, the Goals tab, one generated job from the board, the Bakery Loft move, and a save roundtrip after each step) and checks for `PLAYTHROUGH_OK`. It takes about seven minutes because the drives are real. CI runs both.

`SMOKE_OK` means the pay formula, starter desk, save roundtrip, truck-door clips, and loop-import settings passed. With a display, `CO_SHOTS=/some/dir godot --path . res://tests/playthrough.tscn` also saves a screenshot after each step.

## Controls

- **Click** an item to pick it up. **Click** the floor, a desk, or the top of another item to place it. A red ghost means it would float or overlap.
- **R** rotates 90°. **Shift+R** or the **mouse wheel** rotates 15° while you hold something. **G** toggles the 0.25 m grid. Free placement is the default, and the choice is saved with the volume sliders.
- **Q** / **E** (or the Rotate view button) swing the camera round the room a quarter turn at a time; the two walls nearest the camera cut away so you can always see in. The **mouse wheel** zooms when your hands are empty.
- A footprint shadow follows the ghost and shows exactly where the item will land: sage when it will sit, soft red when it would float or overlap. Landing puffs a little dust.
- The card under the status bar is the room checklist: every goal for the room, ticked as you meet it, with the live Room setup score that feeds the tip.
- Items rest on the floor or on whatever is under them, then settle and freeze so a stack stays put.
- Wall pieces sit 0.12 m off the wall. A soft thunk means the placement landed.
- Click the beige computer to open HomeDesk: Market, Shop, Email, Listings.
- **Map** (or **M**) opens the town map: where you are, which clients have a job pinned, and which offices are for sale. You can take a job or move in from there.
- **Esc** or **Pause** saves and can return to the menu.
- On a drive, **WASD** or the arrow keys drive, **Space** is the handbrake, **H** honks, and **E** loads or unloads at a bay (the HUD has buttons for both). You cannot fail a drive.

## How the slice plays

1. New game opens the character select: Dana Reyes (a laid-off office worker, $120 and a spreadsheet brain), Sol, Hank, Milo or Bea, each with their own look, name, pitch, starting money, a few things of their own for the garage and a perk (`data/narrative/player_characters.json`, `PERKS` in `scripts/game_state.gd`). Read the intro, then unpack the garage.
2. Place the twelve starter pieces (the mug and sticky notes sit on the desk), or press **Place it all for me** and let them find their own spots. The moment the garage is live the clipboard opens itself with the first job on it, so the first drive is minutes away. The HUD's status line keeps a small ledger: the jar, and today's change (`today +$12`) beside it.
3. Open the computer. Trade office goods, or open TropicalForest to order decor. In a wide window the computer keeps a right-hand column of ads, Marge's tip, the drive weather and the day's biggest price mover. Prices move when you press Next day, and a night card sums the day up first: money in and out, jobs, the shop's takings and who was served by hand, trading, decor on the way, goals ticked off, and a teaser for tomorrow's board.
4. The clipboard offers the cafe job after the garage is unpacked. Beside Marge's three story jobs, **Today's board** lists two to four generated moves (`scripts/job_gen.gd`): one of thirty-nine clients with their own line, a venue (cafe, train shop, park, bakery, the Corner Studio, the flat over the bakery, or one of the twenty houses), eight to twenty-two pieces drawn from the catalogue, a twist (cat, sets, mystery bag, ducks, fragile, heavy, or plain), and a drive between two real places in town with its own weather and hazards. The board is seeded from the save and the day, so it survives a reload and changes every morning (press Next day on the computer). The map lists the same board. The clipboard's **Goals** tab holds the contracts in `data/contracts.json`: finish jobs, serve customers by hand, buy decor, take the alley. Each one pays cash and Rep when it ticks over, and Marge mails about it.
5. Pack, pad fragile items, load the truck (heavy boxes on the mustard floor slots), then drive. The truck takes eight boxes, so that is the box limit; tap a loaded pad with nothing selected to take that box back off.
6. The drive is a 3D town seen from a chase camera behind the truck (`data/city.json` lays it out; `drive_course.gd` builds it from meshes): blocks of buildings with inset windows and sills, corner pilasters, a two-step cornice, striped awnings over the shops, chimneys on the gables and the odd rounded corner tower; streets of houses with pitched roofs, front gardens behind hedges, paths to the doors, fences between plots and sheds in the back gardens (`kinds` in `data/city.json` says which blocks are houses or a green); two-lane roads with traffic; the park with the pond and two more greens (a bandstand on one, swings and a slide on the other) with flowerbeds and benches; the tall blocks step back at the top with a hedged terrace; and two alleys you can cut through (`data/city.json` lists them under `alleys`). Honk (**H**) near someone on the pavement and they wave; each friendly honk, up to five a drive, adds a little to the pay and counts toward a goal. The truck starts parked in the client's loading bay. Load up (the boxes hop in off the curb), follow the floating arrow, the dots on the road and the minimap (the whole town with its parks, streets of houses, shops and places as dots, HOME, the load and drop pins by name, the next turn ringed, a fifty-metre scale bar and a compass) to the bay at the other end, **back the truck into it** (nose-first does not count until you reverse; the tail lights come on and it beeps while you back up), and unload. Walls, cars, cones, puddles, bumps, gravel and a hard brake in the rain all cost box condition. Traffic is solid and carries momentum: a car ahead in your lane sets your pace and you sit a car's length behind it, cars keep their own spacing, a car behind you brakes and leans on the horn, and driving into one is a real collision (masses, a little bounce): the heavy truck shoves cars aside, a car hit hard slides and spins before it finds its lane again, and a hard hit spins the truck too. Patrol cars are out every night and by day once the heat is up; spot you twice (fast, in the alley or off the road in front of them) and one gives chase with its siren on, lines up on your rear quarter and PIT-shoves you into a spin. Stop with a patrol on your bumper for three seconds and you are **busted**: no pay, a fine, a Rep star and a strike on the record, and a Busted card instead of results. Keep well ahead for a while and they lose you. The simulation itself is 2D (drive pixels, 32 to the metre), so the condition rules and tests are unchanged.
7. **Models from Blender.** The 56 household pieces and the people's body parts are built by `tools/build_household.py` and `tools/build_figures.py` with Blender's Python module (`pip install bpy`), to the sizes in `data/content.json`, and exported as GLB into `assets/models` (`hh_*`, `fig_*`). Every person is the same jointed rig dressed in those parts, recoloured per person by mesh name (skin, cloth, hair, trousers, shoes), with seven heads (short, bob, ponytail, bun, bald, curly, cap) and four tops (tee, shirt, jacket, apron); kids and elders draw from their own lists, Dana wears a ponytail and a tee under her scarf. `tests/props_sheet.tscn` renders a contact sheet of the lot. The truck's back door swings open and shut in Godot (`open` / `close`, about +105° on Y). The model itself has no animation. None of the models carry animation data, so Biscuit (breathing, the occasional long stretch) and the shop customers (walking bob, swinging arms, a glance around while they wait) are animated in code; rigged art would replace that.
8. Unpack: carry each box in from the truck, click it to tip it out, then place what was inside. Repair a scuff or keep it, and read the pay lines. Base pay is always paid in full. Damage only comes out of the tip.
9. Repeat jobs, trade, and let the shop earn until the Bakery Loft gates are met ($1,500 earned, 3 jobs, Rep 1), then move in from Listings. The board keeps generating after that, so there is always a job to take.

**The team's characters** (`assets/characters/`, `docs/CHARACTERS_README.md`, `scripts/regulars.gd`). Dana's painted portrait is the face on her toasts, the level strip and the intro card (full length, box in hand), and the results card shows an expression for the day: proud of three stars, happy with two, tired after one, surprised if she was shoved. The shop's eight regulars (Steve, Lorna, Kit, Dottie, Theo, Pippa, Rocco, Wren) walk in four times in ten in their own clothes and build, order in their own words for the dish they want, think out loud while they wait, give their verdict when served and say hello and goodbye in their own wordless voices (128 clips, `AudioDirector.play_voice`). The seven named NPC models (Delbert, the vendor, the architect, the flagger, the helper, the band member, the rival) walk past on the street with their own walk and idle clips.

**The truck, loaded properly.** The bed is a grid of eighteen places (`truck` in `data/tuning.json`): two across, three rows from the front wall to the door, three layers to the ceiling. It fills from the front corner outward and upward (a place lights up only when something stands under it and the row ahead is full), wrapped pieces ride on the floor layer, heavy boxes take the floor while there is floor to take (once it is full they ride up top and crush what is under them), and nothing stacks on a sofa. While boxes are still on the truck the camera pulls back to keep the bed in view, and a tap on the truck body brings the next box that can come off. A ramp runs from the tail to the ground; Dana carries each box from the pile, up the ramp and into its place, then comes back down; **Load it all for me** does the lot in the right order, heaviest low. Every piece has a weight (`ItemDB.weight`: its size at a furniture density, heavy pieces more) and a box carries the sum, shown on its button. On the drive a box with more than eighteen kilos on top of it is crushed: extra stress per kilo over, glass most, and the results card counts the pieces crushed under the load. At the new place the truck stands on the road with its tail to the door and the ramp down; boxes come off from the top and the door end first (the one under a stack waits its turn), Dana walks each one down into the box row, and **Unload it all for me** brings the lot in.

**Out the window** (`hospital` in `data/tuning.json`). When an owner's temper breaks and the room has a window, half the time it is worse than a shove: they march you to it and you go through. Glass, a dust cloud on the pavement, and the job ends right there: half pay for what was placed, and St. Bridget's sends the bill. A ground-floor fall is two nights on Ward 3 and $140; from upstairs it is a broken leg, four nights and $360. The ward is a real room: your bed by the window, two neighbours behind curtains, a drip stand, Marge in the chair the first night. Dana lies there crying and thinking out loud, and the chart at the foot of the bed asks what she does with it. **Forgive them**: a rep star, the client sends carnations and half the bill, and from then on every owner takes one more break before they come at you (up to two). **Plan revenge**: a grudge, Sal writes, and the next owner who raises a hand finds you ready: they go down instead, and the small claim that follows is doubled. The clipboard grows a **Payback** tab with six one-shot moves: egg their new front door (cred and heat, and a chance of being seen for a rep star), have Hale & Pruett invoice them for the window and the ward (some pay, some write back), write them up on the town board (a rep star, their name is mud), send Sal's glitter parcel, move them again at twice the pay with nothing in your bag noticed and their hands kept to themselves (the job stays on the board until it is done), or let it go after all. Most moves close the matter. Each **Sleep it off** is a night and a day gone (rent and the board move on), and the discharge note sends you home. All of it survives a save.

**Replay** (`scripts/extras.gd`, `data/gear.json`, `data/achievements.json`, `play` in `data/tuning.json`). Every board job can carry up to two **modifiers**, shown on its card: *Rush* (+35%, kept only if the drive beats half a second a metre plus twenty-five), *Fragile lot* (every piece fragile, +25%), *No helper* (every "for me" button gone, +20%), *Heirloom* (one named piece: $60 whole, $60 and a star if marked) and *Nosy neighbour* (pocketing likelier seen, a clean job $25). Six in ten clients have a **wish** on the card ("Please don't drop anything", "Stack the empty boxes", "Make the room look like a room", "Every piece in its room") worth $20 if granted. A **clean run** (nothing scuffed, chipped or dropped, nobody shoved) grows a streak that pays $5 a job in the streak from the second, capped at ten, and ends at the first mark. The **Movers' League** scores every job (three points a star, two for a clean run) against Hollis & Sons, Two Men and a Van and Pronto Movers, who earn their own overnight; every seventh night the table is settled by letter, the top firm's board jobs pay 10% more for the week, and the table resets. **Gear** on the Books page is bought once and kept: the dolly (the weight on a box counts 80%), moving blankets (wrapped pieces take 40% less of the road), bed racks (the crush line up 8 kg), the tape gun (two more pieces a box), grip gloves (drops 30% rarer) and Marge's lucky charm (the first slip of every job caught). Mid-unpack, once a job, something happens: the client puts the kettle on (say yes and they take one more slip in good part) or a box by the door turns out to be next door's ($15 for walking it round). The **journal** keeps thirty-two entries, from the first job and the first drop to the window, the league and the full kit, each with a letter when it is earned.

**The books** (`scripts/books.gd`, `data/staff.json`). The computer's **Books** page has three sheets. *Ledger*: jobs, job pay, shop takings and trading for today, the last seven days and all time, with rent, wages, savings found and "sundries" beside the jar and everything earned. *The record*: hands (level, pieces handled, dropped), rep, owners (shoves, thrown out, claims, counter-suits), grace (forgiven, grudges, paid back), the shop, the bench, the road, and Marge's tip of the day. *The accountant*: hire Priya Nand (tidy, $45 a night), Len Ocasio (cheap, light-fingered, $25) or Ferdinand Blum (very good, $60, wants a raise every five days). An accountant is paid every night and finds savings on the day's takings (3% to 9%, a point more when paid a fifth over the ask). Pay under the ask and the greedy ones help themselves: each night the chance is how far under you pay times their greed, and the night card only says *Sundries −$X*. **Audit** costs $30 and names the thief, with a letter from Pell & Daughters; **Fire** them and half of it comes back out of their coat. An unpaid wage sends them off, and Len leaves with a cut. The HUD's status line now carries everything earned beside today's change, the objective names the job's step (Pack, Load, Drive, Unpack, 1 to 4), and twenty of Marge's tips rotate through the computer's sidebar, the night card and the record.

**The team's furniture families and character models v2** (28 Oct bundles, `docs/furniture_families/`, `assets/characters/models_v2/`; created by Andrew's Helpers (Grok Bots)). All 202 catalogue families now have a procedural Blender model, `assets/models/hh_cat_<family>.glb`, built at the family's base size with a single tintable material named `way`; `Props.catalog_model` instances it for every `proc:` piece of the family, scales the sized variants, tints `way` to the piece's colour way (copper is never baked: it tints as brass C9A24E), and dresses the named parts (cushion, seat, top, towel, rug...) with the household textures. The nine v2 characters (Dana and the eight regulars) are skinned bodies on a 23-bone humanoid rig with idle, walk and sit clips: `Props.v2_character` wraps one in a person node and the room plays walk on the move, sit when sat, idle otherwise, so Dana wears the team's model in every room and the regulars walk in wearing theirs; the other playable characters keep the code rig. People built from a record take a `skin_index` (the eight approved tones in `Figure.SKIN_TONES`, dealt per person, never by name or role), kids come in three size tiers by age (K1 4 to 6 about 1.05 m, K2 7 to 9 about 1.28 m, K3 10 to 12 about 1.45 m, seat and hip heights scaled), and a `seated` record (Finn's wheelchair) carries its seat height and sits instead of walking; the chair model and seated clips are not built yet. The L07 to L15 sprite set (`assets/ui/driving`, `assets/ui/vfx`, `assets/ui/icons`, `docs/sprites_L07_L15/`) is in the tree and not yet wired: the drive is 3D.

**The big catalogue** (`data/items/*.json`, `tools/gen_items.py`, `docs/ITEMS.md`). Over 1,300 household and office pieces in 200-odd families (mugs, teapots, bar stools, bath mats, teddies, cots, armchairs, bookshelves, trunks, prams, wheelbarrows, filing cabinets, water coolers...), each a few colour ways and a few sizes or named variants with a line of its own, built from a short primitive list the way `data/props.json` pieces are. The item database loads every file in the folder, so the team can add their own in the same format (`python3 tools/check_items.py` validates a file). House moves draw their pieces from it by room, the families' own furniture in the houses comes from it (three to five pieces a room, along three walls), and every office and shop gets a clutter pass: a few floor pieces along the back and left walls and a few things hung up, chosen by what the place is and seeded by the day, each checked against what already stands there.

**Houses** (`scripts/homes.gd`, `data/homes.json`, `docs/HOMES.md`). Fourteen of the board's clients are households moving into one of twenty houses: terraces, semis, a detached, bungalows, flats, townhouses, cottages, lofts, maisonettes and villas, each a blueprint of rooms per floor (hall, lounge, kitchen, dining room, bathroom, bedrooms, study, box room, utility, loft, shed). The house is shown one floor at a time: waist-high interior walls with doorways, a name on every room's floor, and the stairs. **Up to upstairs** / **Down to downstairs** (or a click on the stairs) switches floor, with whatever is in your hand coming along; the boxes stay in the hall. Pieces have a room they belong in (`room` in `data/content.json`), and the results card counts how many landed in the right kind of room, at $3 each, naming the worst misses. The family's own things already stand in the rooms when you arrive (fridge, bath, wardrobe, telly, mower...). Click one and a card offers **Leave it** or **Pocket it**: a small piece goes in the bag for Sal (heat +1, sold from the fence row of the back room), but each one raises the chance someone saw, and being seen costs a quarter of the base pay and a Rep star. Big pieces cannot be walked out with.

**What Dana is earning for** (`scripts/life.gd`, `data/life.json`, `docs/LIFE.md`). The computer's **Life** page is a ladder of her own things, each with a cost, a requirement and a payback: adopt Pickle the terrier ($60; he follows Dana round every room and customers tip 5% more; he eats $4 a day), a rented room (the first job each day tips 10% more), hiring Ray (one more job on the board), a second truck (Ray's run pays a job's wages every night), the flat on Quay Street (vibe +4, and Sam writes), a second counter (half the yard's takings every night), a third truck and Mei, a house with Sam (every job's tips +10%), the depot, a family (Rep +2), three counters, ten trucks, and Corner Office everywhere. Costs climb from $60 to $400,000, so the top is a long way off. The first million earned is a milestone with Marge's letter, and the page tracks the way there. Past it, the page opens **the road to a billion**: a second depot, Dilys's fund (the jar grows 1%, then 2%, then 3% a night), the Harbour Road units, fifty trucks, the county, the rail contract, the tower and every town on the map, with costs from a million to four hundred million; nights can bring an audit, a rival, a strike or a windfall; at a hundred million Grayline offer $250,000,000 for the lot, which ends the empire if you take it; the billion is the end of the story. The night card lists what the other trucks and counters brought in. The sidebar always shows the next rung.

**The client on site** (`scripts/client_party.gd`, `data/clients.json`). Whoever you are moving is there while you pack and unpack: a person, a couple, a family with kids, or a grandparent, standing just inside the door. Every client has a mood: warm ones are delighted by most things, fussy ones measured everything twice, rude ones wonder what they are paying for. They greet you, chat while you work, and comment on what happens: a piece set down (now and then), the right room ("Correct room. Good. Carry on."), the wrong room ("The toaster does not live in the bathroom."), a scuffed piece, a box tipped out, a tower of boxes, the cat, and anything they see go into your bag. Kids have lines of their own. Click one to talk. Twenty of the households have a story (`data/stories.json`, told in full in `docs/STORIES.md`): why they are moving is on the clipboard, they tell the rest in three parts as the room fills (a widow downsizing after forty-six years, twins in their first place without a warden, a family moving in with Grandad so he can keep his house), and the results card closes with the last line of it. The people match the story: the party is an elder, a couple, a family with kids, or three generations, with a mood to suit. The results card otherwise closes with their verdict in their own words.

**Unpacking a box.** Click a carried box and a card shows what is inside, with three ways out: **Take** puts a piece in your hand to place where you like, **Set here** drops it on the nearest free spot behind the box, and **Tip it out** spills the lot on the floor. **Put it all away for me** empties the box, and the HUD's **Unpack it all for me** does every carried box. Packing has the same escape hatch: **Pack the rest for me** boxes, pads and tapes everything that is still loose, and the garage tutorial has **Box it all for me**. None of these is instant: Dana walks to each box, lifts a piece, carries it across the room and sets it down (or carries each loose piece to the open box), and you can watch, or nudge things once she is done.

**Her hands.** Every piece Dana packs or unpacks counts toward a handling level (a dozen a level, five levels, `handling` in `data/tuning.json`). While she is working for you, a piece can slip: shards fly, Dana flinches, the piece is chipped on the spot, and the chance falls from roughly one in five at level 0 to one in fifty at level 5, double for anything fragile. The owner sees every one, and the script climbs with their temper, in their mood's voice (`broken`, `broken_warn`, `broken_last`, `attack`, `throw` in `data/clients.json`, kids included): a wince at the first, a warning and a count at the second, a last word before they come over. Three breaks and they come over, shove her flat, and a card offers **Sue them** or **Let it go**: small claims pays $1,000 to $2,000 the first time. Keep filing and each suit after the first is likelier (a quarter per earlier suit, up to 85%) to come back as a fraud counter-claim at twelve times the amount, two rep stars off, and a letter from Hale & Pruett in the inbox. The results card lists the drops, the award or the counter-suit. Carried boxes line up across the screen so no box ever hides another, and **Stack the boxes** piles the ones still packed into towers of three to clear the floor; click a tower and the top box opens (a lower box sends you to the top). The box card names each piece, says where it goes (floor, wall, on things) and gives it a line of its own.

**The workbench** (`scripts/workshop.gd`, `scripts/workshop_ui.gd`, `data/workshop.json`). Click the bench in the garage or press **Workbench**. *Make* lists recipes that con against your skill the old DAoC way (grey, green, blue, yellow, orange, red): a red make can fail and eat half the materials, yellow and orange teach most, grey teaches nothing. Each success rolls a quality and can bump the skill; 3% are masterpieces. Made things sell to whoever is asking that day (a craft buyer with a tag and a minimum quality pays extra), to the market, or go into the room as decor with **Keep**. *Fix up* takes a worn trade good to restored, then mint, for the market buyers who ask for one ("Only if it's mint, mind"). *Shelf* sells materials. *Journal* keeps the skill and a line about where every sold piece ended up.

**The back room** (`scripts/crime.gd`, `data/crime.json`), a tab on the clipboard. Fencing is a haggle: Sal names a price, you take it or push (twice at most; the third push loses him and adds heat). Night moves are real night drives: dark town, headlights, and patrol cars with light bars that notice a truck that is fast, in the alley or off the road in front of them; each time one spots you, heat goes up. Knock-offs can be caught by a buyer who knows the real thing (a better piece and a better label help); a catch costs heat and a Rep star. The Cover branch has **Close up early** (heat down, a day's custom lost) and **A friend on the force** (a file goes missing). Sal writes as cred climbs, and at fifteen cred the back room offers **The big one**: a whole office after dark at three times the money, with every patrol out.

**Rent.** Hollis Property wants rent every five days after a four-day grace: $80 plus $60 per rung of the shop ladder. It comes out of the jar on the night card; if the jar is short, the shop stays shut until money comes in and settles it, and the landlord writes. Sal runs three short trees bought with street cred: fencing (goods to Sal above market), night moves (a no-questions board job at up to 2.6x), and knock-offs (made things sold as the real thing). Everything adds heat; heat fades daily; past the line there is a visit (fine, confiscation, a Rep slip), and the third visit is a police raid: half the goods gone, a bigger fine, the shop shut for a day, and Marge's email. When a day ends with an empty jar and nothing earned, Sal writes first.

**The ladder in the yard** (`scripts/yard_shop.gd`). As the shop climbs the ladder on the Till, the lawn beside the garage grows the business: a coffee cart with an umbrella, then the building described below as a sandwich bar, a shawarma grill, a café with string lights, and a restaurant on a deck under a pergola with white cloths and lanterns. Customers queue at the counter in the window. The Till's menu follows the rung. About half the customers have a small ask when you click them (extra ice, a warm bun, "is the cat in?"), listed in `data/shop.json` under `asks`; a card offers two replies. Saying yes lifts the tip, saying no trims it, and the customer answers back in a bubble either way. Then you make the order: the **Make it** card (`scripts/make_card.gd`) shows the cup or plate, the recipe in order, and a tray of ingredients with two red herrings. Each right tap adds a layer; a wrong one shakes the cup and costs a little tip. A clean make pays 1.3x the hand tip, one slip pays the plain hand tip, more pays less, and **Let the till do it** hands the order over at the till's half tip. Recipes and ingredients live in `data/shop.json`.

**Ambient life.** Every setting has a three-bay parking lot off the road (`Outdoors._parking`). Cars pull in from the street, park nose-in, and someone steps out and walks to the door: a customer if the shop is taking orders, otherwise a neighbour who wanders in, flops on the beanbag, leans in to a plant, or stops at a shelf. When they are done they walk back to the car and it drives off. Everyone thinks out loud in a thought bubble over their head, drawn from `data/chatter.json` (arriving, waiting, served, browsing by kind, leaving, and passers-by). Passers-by walk the pavement beyond the road (one with a dog). Biscuit breathes and stretches in code.

**The yard.** From the sandwich bar up, the business is a small building: slab, walls, a serving window with the counter and the day's wares in it, a door, shelves of jars and a kitchen inside (toaster, grill with a turning spit, coffee machine and grinder, a pass with heat lamps), a striped awning, a sign on the parapet, a menu board and planters, with tables and chairs out front. The roof covers only the kitchen so the camera looks in over the counter.

**Colour and light.** Every room has a theme in `data/themes.json`: wall, floor and trim colours, a tinted sun, a key light through the window wall, and a low mood glow in the room's accent (terracotta café, dusty-blue train shop, sage park shed, honey bakery, chalk studio, dusk-rose flat). The day has phases: morning until the first job is done, afternoon after it, evening after two, and the evening dims the sun, warms it, and turns the lamps up.

The garage itself is dressed by `scripts/garage_dressing.gd`: a workbench with paint tins, a toolbox and the radio (you can set things on it), a pegboard of tools, a cork board, rafters with string lights and a pendant over the bench, a bike, archive boxes, a broom, a bin, a door mat, planters on the drive, and warm light through the window. All of it is scenery; only the bench blocks.

## TropicalForest and the shop

TropicalForest is a computer app: categories, search, silly reviews, a wishlist, and a daily deal that simply waits until the next in-game day. Standard shipping is free and arrives tomorrow. Express is same-day for a small fee. Toucan Prime, once the jar has earned enough, makes express free.

A courier walks up to the door and rings. Click the box, pop the bubble wrap, and place the item on a free surface. A sealed box can be returned. A placed Forest item can be returned for most of the price or resold to the market. The catalog is `data/forest.json`. It points at the decor models already in the game. Coffee & Kitchen and Seasonal open with the Bakery Loft. Mystery boxes and the occasional freebie are in that same file.

The Shop Manager app (Till) wakes up after the garage is unpacked. It earns only when a fixture from `data/shop.json` is placed: a coffee machine, kettle, lemonade pitcher, cup stack, bookshelf, filing cabinet, fern, or menu board. While the sign is OPEN, an A-board stands by the door and customers walk in from the drive, queue at the counter, and hold their order over their head (a mug, a lemonade glass, a bun). **Click a customer to serve them yourself** for the whole tip; leave them and after a few seconds the till rings them up at half tip. Nobody leaves unserved and nothing fails. Vibe, from plants, lights, and decor, changes how many people come and how they tip. Prices are Cozy, Fair, or Splurge; the Till shows the last sales, who was served by hand, and the OPEN / CLOSED sign. After the cat-cafe job, a cat bed or cushion puts Biscuit on the till. Marge emails a daily summary. Regulars sometimes ask for a TropicalForest item. Add visitors, dishes, or fixtures in `data/shop.json`.

## Project map

- `scenes/main.tscn` boots `scripts/app.gd`.
- `scripts/outdoors.gd` builds the yard around every room (ground, driveway, road, trees, fence or lamp posts, neighbours) from plain meshes with no collision. `scripts/map_ui.gd` is the town map, with its pins in `data/map.json`.
- Autoloads: `ItemDB`, `JobDB`, `GameState`, `SaveManager`, `AudioDirector`.
- `data/tuning.json` holds the locked numbers (damage, snap, tips, office gates, audio gains).
- `data/content.json` is characters, items, venues, and jobs. `data/drives.json` names each drive's pickup and drop-off bay, weather, and hazards (placed by fraction `along` the computed route); `data/city.json` is the town itself and `scripts/city.gd` builds it, routes through it, and runs the traffic loops. `data/forest.json` is the TropicalForest catalog. `data/shop.json` is fixtures, the menu, visitors, and Vibe.
- `data/narrative/` is the intro, cast, emails, item flavor, `player_characters.json`, `character_arcs.json`, `how_to_play.json`, and `credits.json`. Dana (phase `slice`) is the only starter, through Garage Startup to the Bakery Loft. When one of her L1–L6 beats loads, Marge's email lands in the inbox and a short note shows the arrival and personal moment. Sol, Hank, Milo, and Bea arcs are in that file and stay hidden. There is no character select yet.
- `assets/models/` is the diorama kit. A placed prop keeps at most two materials. Props get a box collider at runtime because the glTF files have none. Structural walls are 0.24 m thick (the inner face is 0.12 m, including the garage, whose visual shell is 0.26 m). Rugs and floor tiles are pickable and do not block physics. `npc_*` models get a capsule.
- `assets/ui/driving/mover_truck_topdown_sheet@1x.png` is still the truck placeholder (facing +X, 32 px per meter) until the dedicated top-down truck sprite arrives. The unsuffixed sheet is 2× and is scaled by 0.5 if the @1x file is missing. Hazard tests use the opaque truck, not the padded frame.
- L3 and L5 use the `@1x` driving sprites in `assets/ui/driving/` at 32 px per meter (cones, puddles, ducks, shopfronts, the pond fountain, and the bridge-closed sign). `assets/level-art-manifest.md` maps the L1–L6 props. The Bakery Loft uses the `bakery_loft_*` walls and floors.
- `assets/audio/music/` and the `*loop*` / `*ambience*` clips import with loop on. Drop extra beds in `assets/audio/ambience/` (`.ogg` or `.wav`); those import with loop enabled too.
- Act 1 (L1–L6) follows `assets/audio/level_audio_map.md`: each level plays its music and ambience loop, plus its own sound effects. Rain, skylight rain, the shop clock, and the cafe bed are about +8 dB. Voice lines are still stubs and stay silent.
- `export_presets.cfg` has Linux, Windows, and macOS presets. Windows uses `res://icon.ico`.

Saves go to `user://corner_office_save.json`. Volume sliders live in `user://settings.cfg`.

**Sound from the team.** `docs/ADDITIONS_README.md` lists the audio Andrew's helpers added: nine songs (the lemonade stand has its own, the train shop plays the record store, the loft its own act; Downtown, The Record Store and Parade Band are radio stations; the finale plays at the first million, the parade at ten, downtown at a hundred, the curtain call at the billion, and the credits their reprise), sixteen airs (the stand outdoors, the studio, the flat, the night drive, and a house's air by what it is built of: library for render, lodge for stone, greenhouse for timber, coastal for clapboard) and fifty-odd sounds now wired in: every ingredient on the Make it card sounds like itself (an espresso pull, milk foam, ice, a lemon, sugar, tea, a muddle, a drizzle, whipped topping), the cup goes down and the tip jar rings on a clean make, a bell at the shop door, a chirp for a yes or a no to an ask, the truck's back door and the ramp, boxes scraping into a tower, and the market's buy, sell, profit chime and loss thud.
